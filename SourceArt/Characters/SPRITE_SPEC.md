# Character sprites: Shieldbearer and Archer

2D pixel-art troops for the 3D diorama. Everything here is generated by
`Tools/sprites/` (hand-drawn pixel parts composited in code, no image
generation, no resampling). Re-run with:

```
Tools/.venv/bin/python Tools/sprites/build.py
```

`Tools/.venv` is a local virtualenv (pillow, numpy) that ignores itself; create
it with `python3 -m venv Tools/.venv && Tools/.venv/bin/pip install pillow numpy`.

## Files

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `shieldbearer_color.png`, `archer_color.png` | RGBA sheet, 512 x 1024, transparent background, alpha is 0 or 255 only. Team regions are a neutral grey ramp. |
| `shieldbearer_teammask.png`, `archer_teammask.png` | 8-bit greyscale, same layout, 255 where the pixel is team-tinted. |
| `shieldbearer.json`, `archer.json` | Layout, pivot, timing, facing rule, palette. |
| `previews/lineup.png` | Both troops, both teams, three facings at x8, x2 and true size, plus a mixed skirmish at true size. Look at this first. |
| `previews/<name>_contact_<team>_on_<olive or sand>.png` | Every frame at x6, one row per animation and facing. |
| `previews/<name>_<anim>.gif` | Front, side (red) and back playing together at game speed, x6. |

## Design

Setting: bronze-age Levant. Sandstone, bronze, linen, leather; no plate armour.
Both figures are about 2.5 heads tall with a big head and hands, a 1 px
hue-shifted dark outline, three to five tones per material, warm lights and
cool plum shadows, and no dithering.

**Shieldbearer** (36 px tall, broad). Bronze conical helmet with a team crest
(a tuft from the front and back, a full fore-and-aft crest in profile), short
dark beard, team shoulder mantle that is a full short cloak from behind,
leather cuirass with a bronze pectoral disc, linen kilt with a team hem,
bronze greaves, sandals. Big round team shield with a bronze rim and boss on
the left arm, straight bronze leaf-blade sword in the right hand. A sickle
sword was drawn first and dropped: at true size its curve read as a bow, which
is the one thing this unit must not look like.

**Archer** (34 px tall, slim). Team headcloth held by a cord with a drape over
the neck, beardless, sleeveless linen tunic, leather quiver strap across the
chest, team sash with a hanging tail, bracer, bare legs and sandals. Tall
recurve bow with bronze nocks in the left hand, quiver on the back. No metal on
the body and no shield.

How they stay apart at tactical zoom: gold helmet plus a 13 px team disc
against a team-coloured head plus a brown arc; a wide block against a narrow
column.

Team colour is always carried by the top of the figure (crest or headcloth) and
by one large area (shield and cloak, or headcloth and sash). The player's own
troops are usually seen from behind, so the back views carry the most: the
whole cloak for the Shieldbearer, the whole headcloth and drape for the Archer.

### The bow when shooting

The camera looks down at 43 to 60 degrees, so a bow aimed toward or away from
the camera is edge-on and unreadable if drawn literally. The convention used:

- side: classic profile, bow upright in front, string drawn to the jaw.
- front: the bow as it looks from above, a wide arc in front of the body with
  the arrow pointing down-screen.
- back: the same arc above the head with the arrow pointing up-screen.

## Palette

| Ramp | Light to dark |
| --- | --- |
| skin | `#f2c492` `#d69a68` `#aa6a4c` `#76443e` |
| bronze | `#fff2b2` `#ecbe54` `#be8234` `#82502c` `#523028` |
| linen | `#f6eed6` `#dccca8` `#aa9882` `#70626c` |
| leather / wood | `#c48a54` `#925e38` `#623c2c` `#3e2628` |
| hair | `#60443a` `#3c2a30` `#241a26` |
| team (grey) | `#ffffff` `#bcbcbc` `#898989` `#636363`, outline `#3a3a3a` |

Outline colours are per material (dark red-brown round skin, dark brown round
bronze, plum-grey round linen, near-black round hair and leather). The outline
next to a team region is itself in the team mask, so it becomes a dark navy or
dark maroon instead of a neutral black. Slash smears, dust puffs and
bowstrings are drawn without an outline.

### Team colour

`rgb = lerp(color.rgb, color.rgb * TeamColor * 2, mask)`, in linear space.

The grey ramp is stored so that linear 0.5 (sRGB 188) reproduces `TeamColor`
exactly, linear 1.0 gives a clipped double (the highlight: light blue or light
red), and the two darker greys give the shade and deep shade. The previews
apply exactly this formula with the game's two colours, `(0.05, 0.22, 0.80)`
and `(0.72, 0.07, 0.05)`. Any other `TeamColor` works; very dark team colours
will read as dark cloth, very light ones will lose the highlight step.

## Sheet layout

512 x 1024, cells of 64 x 64, 8 columns by 16 rows. `row = animation * 3 +
facing`, `column = frame`. Unused cells are empty. Every frame keeps at least
one transparent pixel on each side of its cell.

| Rows | Animation | Frames | Loop | Driven by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0-2 | idle (front, side, back) | 4 | yes | time, 4 fps |
| 3-5 | walk | 6 | yes | distance, one cycle per 160 cm |
| 6-8 | attack | 6 | no | attack phase, strike frame = 3 |
| 9-11 | hit | 2 | no | time, 12.5 fps |
| 12-14 | death | 5 | no, hold last | time, 5 frames over `DeathToppleTime` |
| 15 | unused | | | |

- **Pivot**: x = 32, y = 60 px from the top of the cell (bottom-centre, 4 px
  above the cell edge). The soles rest on that line in every upright frame; the
  outline under them is row 60. Rows 61 to 63 are only used by the sword tip in
  the front strike and by gear lying next to a corpse. The build fails if the
  lowest pixel of an upright frame is above that line.
- **Facings**: front faces the camera, back faces away, side faces screen
  right. Mirror side for screen left.
- **idle**: breath lifts the weapon arm, then head and shield, then settles in
  the same order. One pixel of movement, nothing else shifts.
- **walk**: contact, down, passing, contact, down, passing. Body bob is 0, +1,
  -1 px. It is drawn as a jog because the units move at about 4 m/s. Frames 0
  and 3 are the contact poses; snap to one of them when the unit stops.
- **attack**: frames 0-2 are the wind-up, frame 3 is the strike or release,
  frames 4-5 are recovery.
  - Shieldbearer: brace behind the shield, raise, blade at its tallest, strike
    with a lunge and a slash smear, blade carried through, settle.
  - Archer: nock, half draw, full draw, release (string straight, arrow gone,
    draw hand thrown back), follow-through, settle.
  - Suggested mapping. Wind-up progress `PhaseTime / WindupTime`: frame 0 from
    0, frame 1 from 0.34, frame 2 from 0.68. Recovery progress: frame 3 from 0,
    frame 4 from 0.40, frame 5 from 0.72. Frame 3 is therefore the first thing
    shown after `Strike()` and is the pose held through hit-stop.
- **hit**: flinch with eyes shut, then half recovered. Cosmetic; never play it
  over an attack.
- **death**: stagger, buckle and topple, falling, impact with dust, corpse.
  Frames 0 and 1 are authored for all three facings. Frames 2 to 4 are one
  lying pose with the head to the left: face-up for front and side, face-down
  for back. Keep the mirror state the unit had when it died.

## Metadata schema (`<name>.json`)

| Key | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `sheet` | file names, size, cell size, columns, rows, row rule |
| `pivot_px`, `pivot_uv_in_cell` | pivot in cell pixels and as a 0..1 UV inside a cell |
| `figure_height_px`, `cm_per_px`, `quad_size_cm` | scale (see below) |
| `gameplay_reference` | wind-up, recovery, speed, body height and radius read from `SRFeelSettings.cpp` when the sheet was built |
| `facings`, `facing_selection` | what each facing means and the yaw rule |
| `animations.<name>` | `frames`, `loop`, `drive` (`time`, `distance` or `attack_phase`), `fps` or `cycle_distance_cm`, `rows` per facing |
| `animations.attack` | `windup_frames`, `strike_frame`, `recovery_frames`, `windup_phase_map`, `recovery_phase_map` |
| `animations.death` | `hold_last`, `authored_facings`, notes on the shared corpse frames |
| `rows` | flat list: row index, animation, facing, frame count, loop |
| `team` | mask file, boost (2), formula |
| `projectile_origin_cm` | archer only: where the arrow leaves, forward and up from the feet |
| `palette` | every ramp and accent as hex |

## World scale

`ASRUnit::GetBodyHeight()` returns 142 cm for the Shieldbearer and 134 cm for
the Archer (the brief's 170 to 180 cm is the top of the old greybox blade, not
the body). The sprites are drawn at **4 cm per pixel**: 36 px = 144 cm and
34 px = 136 cm, so health bars and aim points keep working unchanged.

- One cell is a 256 x 256 cm quad. The pivot is 16 cm above the quad's bottom
  edge and on its vertical centre line.
- The terrain is 3.5 cm per texel, so a sprite pixel is 1.14 terrain texels:
  the same grain, with the characters a touch chunkier than the ground.
- On a 1080-line viewport a sprite pixel covers about 4.8 screen pixels fully
  zoomed in, 2.35 at the default zoom and 1.3 fully zoomed out; the figure is
  about 170, 85 and 47 screen pixels tall. `previews/lineup.png` shows the x2
  and x1 cases.

## Integration notes for the Unreal phase

### Textures

- Colour sheet: sRGB on, compression `UserInterface2D (RGBA)`, filter
  `Nearest`, mip gen `NoMipmaps`, texture group `2D Pixels (unfiltered)`, never
  stream.
- Mask: sRGB off, compression `Grayscale` (or `Masks`), same filter and mip
  settings.

### Material (one master, for example `M_UnitSprite`)

Masked, two-sided, default lit.

| Parameter | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SpriteSheet`, `TeamMask` | textures | per troop type |
| `Frame` | vector | x = column, y = row |
| `Flip` | scalar | 1 to mirror (unit faces screen left) |
| `TeamColor`, `HitFlash`, `Fade` | as in `Docs/VISUAL_CONTRACT.md` | unchanged contract |

- UV: `((Flip ? 1 - u : u) + Frame.x) / 8`, `(v + Frame.y) / 16`.
- Base colour: the team formula above, then lerp toward `HitFlashColor` by
  `HitFlash`. Opacity mask: sheet alpha, multiplied by the existing dither on
  `Fade`.
- Lighting: sprites should take the golden-hour light and the shadows of trees
  and rocks, but must not shade like a tilted card. Override the normal with a
  constant (world up blended toward the camera), specular 0, roughness 1, and
  keep a small unlit floor (the role `ShadowLift` plays in `M_UnitGreybox`) so
  units stay readable inside cast shadows.
- Sampling: point sampling is correct at integer scale. The camera zooms
  continuously, so at fractional scale pure nearest sampling will shimmer as
  units move. If that shows, switch the sampler to bilinear and snap the UV to
  texel centres with a one-screen-pixel blend:
  `uv_px = uv * size; uv = (floor(uv_px) + 0.5 + clamp((frac(uv_px) - 0.5) / fwidth(uv_px), -0.5, 0.5)) / size`.
  The 1 px transparent border round every frame keeps that safe.
- Drive `Frame`, `Flip`, `HitFlash`, `Fade` and `TeamColor` through custom
  primitive data rather than one dynamic material instance per unit, so all
  units share one material and can later move to an instanced mesh.

### Billboard

The camera pitch is 43 to 60 degrees. An upright card would be squashed to
between 50 and 73 percent of its height, so the sprite must face the camera:

- Give the quad absolute rotation and set it every frame to the camera's
  rotation (the same value for every unit), with the pivot at the unit's feet.
  The actor still turns with `FacingYaw` for gameplay; the sprite does not.
- A camera-facing card leans back, so its head sits roughly 100 to 125 cm
  behind its feet and can sink into a rock or terrace wall behind the unit. Pull vertices
  toward the camera along their view ray in world position offset, in
  proportion to height on the card. That does not move them on screen and
  gives the card the depth of an upright one.
- Fallback if that proves fiddly: a yaw-only upright card stretched vertically
  by `1 / cos(pitch)`. Correct at the feet, costs nothing in the shader, but
  the card is physically 1.4 to 2 times too tall for anything that overlaps it.

### Shadows

The visible card should not cast. Add a second quad with the same material and
frame parameters, hidden in game with `bCastHiddenShadow`, upright, and yawed
to face the sun's horizontal direction. The sun then throws the real silhouette
of the current frame on the ground, which is the look in the references. A
soft blob under the feet is the cheap fallback and is worth keeping anyway for
contact.

### Facing

With camera yaw fixed at 0, +X is up-screen and +Y is screen right. Let
`rel = FacingYaw - CameraYaw`, wrapped to -180..180:

- `|rel| <= 45`: back.
- `|rel| >= 135`: front.
- otherwise side, with `Flip = 1` when `rel < 0`.

Hold the current facing until the yaw is 10 degrees past a boundary so units
do not flicker at 45 degrees. Using `rel` rather than the raw yaw keeps this
right if the camera ever rotates.

### Expected changes in `ASRUnit`

- `BuildVisuals`: replace the greybox parts, `WeaponPivot` and `NockedArrow`
  with the sprite quad and the shadow quad under `VisualRoot`. Keep `Ring`.
- A small per-type table (sheet, mask, rows, strike frame, walk cycle length);
  the JSON has every number, or hard-code them next to `FSRUnitStats`.
- `StepVisual`: pick the animation in this order: dead, attack (wind-up or
  recovery), hit (only while not attacking, for `HitFlashTime`), walk (speed
  above a small threshold), idle. Then set `Frame` and `Flip`.
  - walk: `frame = floor(frac(DistanceWalked / 160) * 6)`. `WalkPhase` already
    integrates distance; one cycle is `2 * PI * 160 / (2 * StrideLength)` of it.
  - attack: the phase maps above. `Strike()` already fires when the wind-up
    ends, so damage and the arrow spawn land on frame 3 with no timing change.
- Remove what the sprite now does itself: breathing scale, walk bob, lean and
  waddle, weapon rotation. Keep the spawn pop-in scale, the acknowledge hop,
  the hit flash, hit-stop and the melee lunge offset. The hit squash is
  optional; keep it small.
- `StepDeathVisual`: no topple rotation. Play the five frames over
  `DeathToppleTime`, hold the last, freeze facing and flip at the moment of
  death, then fade with `Fade` and sink only a few centimetres.
- `GetMuzzleLocation`: 40 cm forward and 84 cm up matches the bow hand.
- `GetBodyHeight`, radii, ring sizes and health bars need no change.

## Limits

- Three facings only; no diagonals. A unit moving diagonally shows the nearest
  of the three.
- The mirrored side view swaps handedness and light direction, as in any
  flipped sprite set.
- The front and back archer shots use the top-down bow convention described
  above rather than a literal view.
- Death frames 2 to 4 are shared between front and side.
