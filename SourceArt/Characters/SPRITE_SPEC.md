# Character sprites: Shieldbearer and Archer

Pre-rendered pixel-art troops for the 3D diorama. Each character is modelled,
rigged and animated in Blender by script, rendered from eight fixed directions,
and reduced to sprite sheets with a deliberate pixel-art finish. Nothing is
downloaded or generated by an image service; every file here can be rebuilt
from `Tools/blender/`.

This replaces the first, hand-pixelled set (64 px cells, three facings). That
generator (`Tools/sprites/`) and its sheets are gone; this is the only source.

## Rebuild

```
Tools/blender/build_all.sh                 # everything, about two minutes
Tools/blender/build_all.sh --elev 40       # same, rendered for a different camera elevation
```

Needs Blender 5.3 at `/Applications/Blender.app` (override with `BLENDER=`)
and `Tools/.venv` with pillow and numpy (the script creates it if missing).
The steps, which can also be run one at a time:

| Step | Command | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model, rig, animate | `Blender -b -P Tools/blender/build_character.py -- <name>` | `blender/<name>.blend`, `fbx/<name>.fbx` |
| Render | `Blender -b blender/<name>.blend -P Tools/blender/render_sprites.py -- [options]` | `_build/<name>_cells.npz` (not committed) |
| Sheets | `Tools/.venv/bin/python Tools/blender/make_sheets.py` | `<name>_color.png`, `_teammask.png`, `_normal.png`, `<name>.json` |
| Previews | `Tools/.venv/bin/python Tools/blender/make_previews.py [--no-gif]` | `previews/` |
| Rig report | `Blender -b blender/<name>.blend -P Tools/blender/check_rig.py` | IK reach errors, walk foot travel |
| Elevation study | `Tools/.venv/bin/python Tools/blender/elevation_compare.py 45 40 30 22` | `previews/elevation_compare.png` |

Render options: `--elev` (camera elevation in degrees, default 30),
`--cm-per-px` (1.75), `--cell` (160), `--pivot x,y` (80,118), `--ss`
(supersampling, 4), `--samples` (24), and `--anims`, `--facings`, `--frames`
to render a subset while iterating.

Source files: `srkit.py` (primitives, mesh builder, rig, posing),
`sranim.py` (hand authoring in character space, head look-at, run cycle),
`char_shieldbearer.py` and `char_archer.py` (the two designs and every pose),
`srmats.py` (materials and palette ramps), `srrender.py` (camera, lights,
passes, the reduction to the sprite grid), `srpost.py` (ramps, cleanup,
outline, team mask, normal map).

## Design

Setting: bronze-age Levant. Bronze, leather, linen; no plate armour. Both are
stocky and grounded, a little over three heads tall, with big fists, heads and
weapons, and faces that are suggested rather than drawn.

**Shieldbearer** (142 cm). Conical bronze helmet with a dark brow band, four
ribs, ear flaps, neck guard and nasal bar, and a team horsehair plume falling
back from the apex. Full auburn beard. Banded bronze scale cuirass with a
leather yoke and shoulder caps, linen sleeves and kilt with a team hem, a
leather apron strap with a bronze disc, bronze greaves, leather boots. A short
team cape with a pale hem carries the colour from behind. Large round shield
on the left arm: team face with a paler ring, bronze rim and boss, wooden
back. Broad leaf-bladed bronze short sword in the right hand.

**Archer** (134 cm). Team headcloth held by a dark cord, with a drape over the
nape. Short dark beard. Sleeveless linen tunic, team sash with a hanging tail,
team hem, a leather quiver strap across the chest, bracer on the bow arm,
leather leg wraps and boots. Quiver on the back with fletchings at the right
shoulder. Tall recurve bow with pale horn tips in the left hand. No metal on
the body and no shield.

Telling them apart at tactical zoom: a wide bronze-and-shield block against a
narrow pale column with a blue or red head and a bow arc.

Team colour sits at the top of each figure (plume, headcloth) and on one large
area (shield and cape, or headcloth, sash and hem).

## Look and pixel treatment

One EEVEE render per frame at 4x the cell size, with every material swapped to
white clay plus a material-id output. Passes: lighting, normal, depth, AO, id.

1. **Reduce** (`srrender.reduce_cell`). Each sprite pixel takes one material,
   the commonest among its fully covered sub-samples, and the mean lighting,
   normal and depth of that material's sub-samples only. Nothing is blended
   across a material edge or with the background, so edges stay crisp. The
   bowstring and arrow shafts win at a lower count so they stay one pixel wide.
2. **Ramps** (`srpost.stylise`). The lighting value, darkened by AO and with a
   per-material highlight from the normal, picks a band of that material's
   hand-chosen ramp in `srmats.py`. Ramps shift hue: plum and cool in the
   darks, warm and pale at the top. The colour of a pixel is therefore always
   one of a fixed set, on every frame. Shieldbearer 50 colours, Archer 42.
3. **Cleanup**. Lone pixels of a material take their commonest neighbour;
   a shade no same-material neighbour shares takes the nearest one that is.
4. **Interior lines**. Where the surface behind is 5 cm or more further away,
   the far pixel drops two bands. This separates arm from torso and leg from
   leg without drawing black lines inside the figure.
5. **Outline**. One pixel outside the silhouette, dark plum `#1b1220`. Next to
   a team region the outline is grey and in the team mask, so it becomes dark
   navy or maroon. Strings, arrows and the strike smear get no outline.

The cuirass rows carry a cell pattern in the lighting pass (rest-pose
coordinates, so it sticks to the surface), which gives the mottled metal.

The lighting is baked: warm key from the left and toward the camera, cool
fill from the right, a rim from behind. The colour sheet alone is the intended
look. The normal sheet is for letting the scene sun add to it, not replace it
(see the material below). No flat albedo pass is shipped.

### Team colour

`rgb = lerp(color.rgb, color.rgb * TeamColor * 2, mask)`, in linear space.

The grey ramp runs from linear 0.04 to 0.45, so the brightest band is close to
`TeamColor` itself and the body of the cloth is about half of it: a deep royal
blue and a deep crimson with the game's `(0.05, 0.22, 0.80)` and
`(0.72, 0.07, 0.05)`. The paler trim ramp runs higher. Previews apply exactly
this formula.

## Scale, camera and pivot

- **Elevation**: rendered from 30 degrees above the horizon. The gameplay
  camera in `SRFeelSettings.h` is 30 degrees zoomed in and 46 zoomed out.
  30 matches the close view exactly, where the sprite is examined, and keeps
  the face, torso and legs readable; at 40 and above the helmet top takes over
  the figure (`previews/elevation_compare.png`). Checked in the engine at the
  far zoom and kept. Re-render with `--elev` if the camera settles elsewhere.
- **Scale**: 1.75 cm per pixel, measured in the camera plane. A cell is a
  280 x 280 cm quad facing the camera. The figure is rendered at its true
  height, so `GetBodyHeight()` (142 and 134 cm), health bars and aim points
  line up without change.
- **Size**: the Shieldbearer stands 83 px to the helmet tip and 90 px with the
  plume, outline included; the Archer 75 px.
- **Pivot**: pixel (80, 118) from the top-left of every cell, UV (0.5, 0.7375)
  inside the cell. It is the actor origin on the ground between the feet. The
  quad therefore reaches 140 cm left and right of the unit, 206.5 cm up the
  screen and 73.5 cm down.
- Every frame keeps at least 5 px of empty border inside its cell.

## Sheet layout

3840 x 2560, cells of 160 x 160, 24 columns by 16 rows. Six animation blocks
of 8 columns by 8 rows, three across and two down:

| | columns 0-7 | columns 8-15 | columns 16-23 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rows 0-7 | spawn | idle | walk |
| rows 8-15 | attack | hit | death |

Inside a block, row = facing in the order S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, E, SE and
column = frame. `column = origin_cell[0] + frame`,
`row = origin_cell[1] + facing_index`; `origin_cell` is in the JSON per
animation. Unused cells are empty. All eight facings are rendered; nothing is
mirrored, so the shield stays on the left arm.

| Animation | Frames | Loop | Driven by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| spawn | 6 | no | time, 14 fps; the unit is active on frame 5 |
| idle | 8 | yes | time, 6 fps |
| walk | 8 | yes | distance, one cycle per 124 cm |
| attack | 8 | no | attack phase; strike frame 3 (Shieldbearer), 5 (Archer) |
| hit | 3 | no | time, 18 fps |
| death | 8 | no, hold last | time, 12 fps |

- **spawn**: deep crouch behind the shield (or on the bow), rise, overshoot
  tall with the weapon lifted, settle, guard.
- **idle**: authored in whole-pixel beats so the shading never crawls. Torso
  rises on frame 2 and drops on frame 5, the hands follow one frame late, the
  weapon tilts and settles, plume and cloth sway.
- **walk**: a run, because the units move at about 4 m/s. Frame 0 is the left
  foot's contact, 1 down, 2 passing, 3 up, 4 the right foot's contact, and so
  on. The planted foot moves back 15.5 cm per frame, which is exactly the
  distance the body covers per frame, so the feet do not slide
  (`check_rig.py` prints this). Hips counter-rotate against the chest, the
  shield leads, cape and plume bounce a beat late.
- **attack, Shieldbearer**: frames 0-2 coil (weight back, sword up behind the
  shoulder, front foot lifting), frame 3 is the strike (lunge onto the front
  foot, torso unwound, arm and blade at full reach, with a smear along the
  blade's arc), frames 4-7 follow through low across the body and return to
  guard.
- **attack, Archer**: frame 0 nock, 1-3 draw to the jaw, 4 anchor, frame 5 is
  the release (string forward of rest, arrow gone, draw hand thrown back, bow
  rocking forward), 6-7 follow through and recover.
- **hit**: recoil, hunch, most of the way back to guard. Cosmetic; do not play
  it over an attack.
- **death, Shieldbearer**: knocked back, staggers and lets the sword go, knees
  buckle, drops to the knees and loses the shield, topples onto his back,
  bounce, settle, corpse with shield and sword beside him.
- **death, Archer**: knocked back, folds over a hand at the chest, drops the
  bow, knees, falls on his face, bounce, settle, corpse with the bow beside
  him and the quiver uppermost.

## In the game

Implemented. The full description of the Unreal side (assets, material, card, shadow, frame selection,
knobs) is `Docs/VISUAL_CONTRACT.md`, "Troop sprites"; this is the short version and what the sheets must
keep true for it to work.

### Import

```
Tools/.venv/bin/python Tools/sprites/gen_sprite_cards.py     # card meshes sized from <name>.json, the arrow sprite
Tools/ue_py.py Tools/import_character_sprites.py             # /Game/SR/Characters
Tools/ue_py.py -c "ONLY=['sprite']; exec(open('<repo>/Tools/build_visual_materials.py').read())"
```

| Sheet | Asset | sRGB | Compression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `_color` | `T_<Name>_Color` | on | `UserInterface2D` (uncompressed BGRA8) |
| `_teammask` | `T_<Name>_TeamMask` | off | `Grayscale` |
| `_normal` | `T_<Name>_Normal` | off | `VectorDisplacementmap` (uncompressed), not `Normalmap`; green not flipped |

All nearest, no mips, never streamed, group `2D Pixels`.

### What the code takes from the metadata

`Source/SovereignRealms/Units/SRSprite.cpp` holds the numbers it needs as constants, copied from the JSON:
block origins and frame counts, `strike_frame`, `windup_phase_map`, `recovery_phase_map`, the figure's top
row (for health bars and picking) and `projectile_origin_cm`. `Tools/sprites/gen_sprite_cards.py` reads
`quad_size_cm` and `pivot_uv_in_cell` for the card mesh. The material instances carry `SheetCells` (24, 16),
`CellPx` (160, 160) and `PivotV` (0.7375). **If a re-render changes the layout, the cell size, the pivot or
the phase maps, update those three places.** A re-render that only changes the pixels needs the import alone.

### How a frame is chosen

As specified above ("Driven by"), in this order: death, spawn, attack (wind-up or recovery), hit (standing
only), walk, idle. Checked in the running game with `sr.ShotOn`: the frame on screen when melee damage lands
is attack frame 3, and when the arrow spawns it is attack frame 5.

- Walk uses the distance the unit actually moved, one cycle per `WalkCycleDistance` (124 cm, Sovereign
  Feel > Animation). The old `StrideLength` setting is gone; if the run cycle is re-authored for another
  `STEP`, change `WalkCycleDistance` to four times it.
- Death takes `DeathAnimTime` (0.67 s = 8 frames at 12 fps), replacing `DeathToppleTime`.
- Spawn takes `SpawnPopTime` (0.38 s) for its six frames, a little faster than the 14 fps it was authored at.
- `MeleeLungeDistance` is 12 cm on top of the 13 cm the strike frame carries.
- The strike smear in frame 3 is kept; the game adds no slash effect of its own.

### How it is shown

- One camera-facing 280 x 280 cm card per unit with its pivot on the actor origin. A world position offset
  gives it the depth of an upright figure with the part below the pivot lying on the ground.
- `M_UnitSprite`: masked, lit. `colour * Relight` is lit by the scene through the normal sheet,
  `colour * (1 - Relight) * BakedLight` is emissive. In the level `Relight` 0.6, `BakedLight` 3,
  `NormalStrength` 0.7 (Sovereign Feel > Visuals > Sprites).
- Shadow: a hidden shadow-only card showing the row for the unit's yaw **relative to the sun**, because the
  sun's 33 degree elevation is almost the 30 the sheets were rendered from. Keep the render elevation near
  the sun's if either changes.
- Elevation: judged in the engine at all three zooms. The 30 degree render matches the close view and still
  reads at the 46 degree far zoom, where the figures are about 80 px tall; the sheets were not re-rendered.

## Limits

- Bow shots straight toward or away from the camera (S and N) are the weakest
  read: the bow is close to edge-on and the draw arm is partly behind the
  body. The bow is canted and the release frame rocks it forward to help, and
  the diagonals are clear.
- The Shieldbearer's cape is one rigid piece on one bone. It bounces but does
  not fold.
- Lighting is baked from the left. Units facing W still have their highlights
  on the left, as with any sprite set.
- The idle is authored in whole pixels for a 30 degree render. At another
  elevation its bob is no longer exactly one pixel.
- The strike smear is baked into frame 3. If the game adds its own slash
  effect, remove the `smear` primitive in `char_shieldbearer.py`.
- In the game the image is temporally anti-aliased: a running unit is a little
  softer than a standing one.
