# Gameplay systems

How the match works, where each rule lives, the balance as tested, and the performance work. Interface: `Docs/UI.md`.
Art and rendering contract: `Docs/VISUAL_CONTRACT.md`. Every number below is a setting in **Project Settings > Game >
Sovereign Feel** (`Source/SovereignRealms/Common/SRFeelSettings.{h,cpp}`, overrides in `Config/DefaultGame.ini`).

## The match

- **Maps**: two, chosen on the New Game screen (and recorded in every saved game). Both are generated by
  `Tools/world/gen_battlefield.py --map two | four` (plans in `Tools/world/maps.py`; pictures and checks under
  `Saved/MapPreview/`: `layout.png`, `report.txt`, and `layout4.png`, `report4.txt`). Each is its own level with its own
  assets and its own numbers on an `ASRMapInfo` actor (bounds, starts, camera height, minimap), which the game copies
  over the global settings when the level starts (`ASRMapInfo::ApplyToSettings`, from `ASRGameMode::InitGame`); the list
  the menu offers is Sovereign Feel > Maps. `sr.StartMatch [difficulty] [two|four]`, `sr.Map`.
  - **The land is real terrain**, not terraces: one smooth surface (a 1 m heightfield cut into an irregular triangle
    mesh, `Tools/world/terrain.py`), with slopes instead of cliffs and ramps. Ground meant for walking is no steeper
    than about 1 in 5.7; a barrier is a mountainside (1 in 1 or steeper, shown as rock by the terrain material, which
    keeps turf on what troops can walk). Roads are graded into the hills (never steeper than 0.175) and are the
    easy ways up; anywhere the grass is, troops can go. Relief: the rivers lie at 0, the rolling country at 5 to
    9 m, the kingdoms' tables at 17.5 m, the hills troops hold at 23 to 26 m, mountains inside the field to 33 m (the
    Riverlands) and 55 m (the Marches), the far wall beyond the field to 115 m, and the Black Meres 7 m *under* the
    rivers.
  - **Level ground is building ground.** A footprint must be level to 30 cm, and buildings cannot level it, so the
    plans provide it: each kingdom's **table** (about 9000 m2, exactly level, room for a town: over 600 farm-sized
    spots within the castle's 38 m), the benches and crowns of the hills, the islands, the High Moor, a level pad
    under every vein (room for the mine and a tower beside it) and every ruin, and **greens** out in the country (by
    the lane bridges, the fords, the crossroads). On a hillside the placement ghost says "The ground is too uneven".
  - **Water** is deep (a wall) wherever it is more than 14 cm deep (`WadeDepth`), whatever its height: the level's
    water sheets carry the tag `SRWater` and the grid measures the depth under them, so a tarn on a hill or a mere
    in a hollow blocks like the river. A ford's or a marsh's bed lies 8 cm under: walkable.
  - **THE RIVERLANDS** (`two`, `/Game/Maps/L_Battlefield`, ±240 m; castles 310 m apart, about 273 m start to
    start on foot: 70 s for a Shieldbearer). Point-symmetric: the enemy's half is the player's turned 180 degrees.
    - **River**: from the lake beyond the far-left corner to the lake beyond the near-right one, in a valley of its
      own. Deep; crossed at **ford A** (top-left) and **ford B** (bottom-right), braided reaches 20 m wide, and
      over the **shrine island** at the centre by a 7 m bridge from each bank (the king's road).
    - **The brook**: each kingdom's second stream, in a ravine. It falls from the Highland's tarn 16 m down a rock
      face into a pool and runs to the river just above the island; crossed at the **lane bridge** and the **upper
      ford**. Between brook and river: the **water meadow** (marsh, a vein, an old tower), where a foe who has
      forced ford A still has a crossing to make, or the long way over the Highland.
    - **The table**: each kingdom's broad level height, with a long open hillside down toward the river (the way
      an army comes) and mountainsides behind and beside it.
    - **The Heights** (23 m bench, 26 m crown): the kingdom's own hill beside the table, by its own road. The safe
      vein on the bench, a **relic** on the crown.
    - **The Overlook**: a whaleback of rock between the centre lane and the stone lane (22 m; nobody climbs it).
    - **The Highland** (16.5 m bench, 19.5 m crown): the upland beyond the brook, as far from one castle as from
      the other. Two veins, the tarn and its fall, a relic on the crown; a hillside on the kingdom's side and one
      into the water meadow.
    - **The Black Mere**: a deep round hollow in the downs by the Heights, dark water at the bottom, mist over it.
    - **Landmarks**: the shrine (a paved dais ringed by standing stones) on the island; the **colossus** (a king of
      the old realm, broken off at the knees) by the king's road on the player's side and his three **obelisks**
      on the enemy's; the **wyrm's bones** in one water meadow and the **field of swords** (an old battle's
      leavings) in the other; the old **aqueduct** striding over each brook's mouth; three **barrows** on the stone
      lane; a stone circle; the fallen bridge above ford A; a jetty and a drowned tower in each lake arm; the old
      kingdom's arches and walls along the roads; watch posts and cairns at the crossings.
    - **Veins**, twelve: under each starting mine, on each Heights' bench (safe), two on the shrine island
      (contested), one in each water meadow, two on each Highland. **Relics**, four (each Heights' and Highland's
      crown); **old towers**, six (the plain, the water meadow, the stone lane).
  - **THE FOUR MARCHES** (`four`, `/Game/Maps/L_Battlefield4`, ±360 m: 71% longer and nearly three times the
    ground). Four kingdoms on tables in the four corners; the same from every corner (quarter turns).
    - **The High Moor**, the middle and the main contested ground: a level height 23 m up and 118 m across, with
      a hillside down toward each kingdom (the four high roads meet on it) and a rock face over each river. On it
      the **Great Circle** (two rings of standing stones, avenues, obelisks) round the great **relic**, four veins
      and four tarns.
    - **Four rivers** fall from the moor's tarns into pools and run out along the axes to a lake at each edge,
      each parting two neighbours. Each is crossed at an **inner ford** near the moor and at an **island** midway
      (a bridge from each bank): the contested ground between two neighbouring corners, with two veins and a
      **relic** on the island and an **old tower** on each bank.
    - **Each march** (a quarter of the map): the table in the corner under its mountains; **the Shoulder** (a bench
      by the kingdom, the safe vein); **the Watch** (a hill over the river with a relic on its crown); two veins out
      toward the moor; three old towers; the Teeth and other mountains nobody climbs; a Black Mere; greens by the
      hub, the moor's foot, the ford and the island's bridge. A ring of roads joins the four hubs through the
      islands, and an inner square joins them over the fords.
    - **Landmarks**: a different wonder on each island (the colossus, the wyrm's bones, the obelisks, the field of
      swords) and by each high road; barrows, a stone circle, an aqueduct over each river, a jetty and a drowned
      tower in each lake.
    - **Veins**, 28; **relics**, 11 (the Great Circle, four islands, four Watches, two fallen keeps); **old
      towers**, 16.
    - **Played one against one for now.** The player holds the near-left corner, the enemy commander the
      far-right (633 m apart on foot: 2 min 40 s for a Shieldbearer). The other two kingdoms are **fallen**: their
      tables stand empty but for the ruin of the keep, which is a relic (a Relic Tower can be raised on it), their
      home veins are free, and all their ground can be built on. See "Four kingdoms" at the end of this file for
      what real four-sided play needs.
  - **Wildlife** (`ASRWildlife`, Sovereign Feel > World > Wildlife): herds of red deer (a stag, hinds, calves) on the
    open ground away from the kingdoms. They graze, look round and drift about as a herd, and bolt from any troop
    that comes within 10 m, settling again once left alone. Cosmetic: no collision, not selectable; hidden in the fog
    of war like the foe's troops, so a bolting herd does not give an unseen army away (one in your sight does).
  - Elsewhere: boulders that block, woodland (stands of firs whose trunks troops thread between, and firs on the
    mountainsides where nobody walks), rocky tors strewn over the open country.
  - Saves made before these maps (the terraced ±210 m field) cannot be loaded: their positions are inside the new
    hills. They stay on disk and in the list, marked "Old map".
- **Win / lose**: the side whose castle falls loses (`USRBattleSubsystem::NotifyBuildingDestroyed`).
- **Economy**: farms make **food**, which buys **troops** (trained at a **Barracks**: Shieldbearers, or an
  **Archery Range**: Archers; the castle no longer trains); mines make **gold**, which buys **buildings**. Each
  farm and mine has workers (miners walk into the adit and come out with ore; farmhands hoe the field and carry
  sheaves to the cottage) and the income lands when they deliver (`ASRWorker`, `ASRBuilding::Deliver`).
- **Population**: the castle houses 8 troops, each farm 4, capped at 36 (`MaxPopulation`). Fewer, weightier troops:
  each one matters.

Starting stock: 320 gold, 200 food, 3 Shieldbearers + 2 Archers each side.

**Recruiting**: a barracks, range, stable, workshop or the Citadel has no queue limit (`MaxRecruitQueue` 0); each
recruit is paid for and housed when queued. Click (or Q / E / R / T / Y, J for the Scout) queues one, Shift queues 5 (`RecruitBatch`),
Ctrl or Option queues 10 (`RecruitBatchLarge`); a batch takes as many as can be paid for and housed.
A troop's housing is held from the moment it is paid for: the top bar's population counts troops in the field plus
those in training (its tooltip splits them), so it never promises room that is already taken. The panel groups a long
queue into runs ("Archer x9"); the badge over the building shows the whole queue's size.

**Building states** (`ESRBuildingState`; meshes `SM_Bld_<Name>_Site / _Damaged / _Ruin` from the Blender scripts): a
construction site (scaffold, footings, stone and timber stacks) with the walls rising course by course inside it;
complete; battle-damaged below 50% health (holed roofs, charred rafters, missing merlons, soot, debris); and after the
collapse a ruin of low rubble and wall stubs (the ruin of a building raised in the match sinks away after 40 s).

## Building

`USRGridSubsystem` (`Battle/SRGridSubsystem.*`) samples the field into 1 m cells at the start of play: ground height,
water (by its depth under the level's `SRWater` sheets), the hidden blocker boxes (rocks, trunks, ruins: one instanced
actor tagged `SRBlocker`), gold veins, and building footprints while they stand. **A cell is open ground only if all
of it is**: where the ground changes height the cell's four corners are traced as well, and a spread taller than a
step (45 cm) marks it as an edge cell: too steep to walk (a mountainside, a bank; closed to walking and building;
troops are not shoved or lured onto it either: `USRBattleSubsystem::IsSteep`). Ground with all four neighbours within
20 cm is open without the corner traces, and ground between two neighbours more than 70 cm off the same way is closed
without them: the two cases that make up nearly all of a smooth map. A footprint takes every cell it reaches into. A
building can be placed when every footprint cell is dry, unblocked and within 30 cm of level, one free cell separates
it from other buildings and no enemy troop stands on it (mines must also snap onto a free vein).

The grid is the one truth for where troops can go. It also keeps the field's **connected ground** (which cells can be
walked between; re-read when a building goes up or falls), so a click on a crag, in the water or across a cliff sends
troops to the nearest spot they can actually reach. `sr.GridCheck` logs the pieces of ground and whether each castle
gate and gold vein can be walked to; `sr.PathTest X0 Y0 X1 Y1` plans a route and logs its corners; with
`bDebugDrawPaths` on (Sovereign Feel > Grid) the routes and the edge / near-wall cells round the camera are drawn.
The map generator runs the same rules on its plan (`Tools/world/gen_battlefield.py`, `validate`): a map with a vein,
a hill top or a river crossing nobody can walk to is not written; ways that exist only hard against a wall are
reported as NARROW warnings.

**Relics and old towers: repaired by Builders.** The field has two kinds of ruin (`FSRRelic`, found by
`USRGridSubsystem::Build`; marks on the minimap while free: relics pale blue, old towers grey):
- **relics** (actors tagged `SRRelic` by the level builder; four on the Riverlands, eleven on the Marches), shown as
  the ruin of a Relic Tower (`SM_Bld_RelicTower_Ruin`, in place of the level's marker there);
- **old towers** (tagged `SRRuinTower`: the broken square towers; six and sixteen).
Select Builders and right-click a ruin (the pointer says what it becomes and the price): it is paid for, laid as a site
and the Builders go and raise it (`ASRPlayerController::IssueRepairRuin`). A relic becomes a **Relic Tower** (900 gold +
300 food, 75 s, 4500 health; model `Tools/blender/bld_relictower.py`: an ancient eight-sided tower with a gallery and a
beacon): two archers (the fighting top and the gallery), each loosing every 1.1 s for 85, reach 42 m, and it sees as
far. It is not in the build menu: a relic is the only way to one. An old tower becomes a **Watchtower** for
`RuinRepairCost` (60%) of its price (a Watchtower placed from the build menu near one also snaps onto it). While a
tower stands the ruin is gone; when it is cancelled or its rubble cleared, the ruin is back and free for either side.

**Siege Tower** (Defence tab; 380 gold + 120 food, 40 s, 2600 health; one, +1 for every two Citadel levels; model
`Tools/blender/bld_siegetower.py`: a squat stone tower with a mangonel on its deck). Every 4.2 s it lobs a stone that
lands on its target's spot and hurts every foe within 2.6 m (70 at the middle, less toward the edge); reach 30 m, but
nothing closer than 5 m (`SplashRadius`, `MinRange`). `sr.Build siege X Y`.

**The film behind the main menu.** There is no startup movie: the game opens straight on its main menu, and
`Content/Movies/LoadingBattle.mp4` (36.5 s, silent) loops behind the title and the buttons, filling the screen
(`ASRHUD::UpdateMenuFilm` / `DrawMenuFilm`: a `UMediaPlayer` on the file, a `UMediaTexture` drawn first on the canvas,
cover fit; a veil and a top and bottom vignette keep the lettering readable). The world behind it is not rendered while
it shows. It is closed and released when a match starts and opened again on returning to the menu; if the file is
missing or cannot be played the live realm is the backdrop as before. `Content/Movies` is staged beside the pak
(`DirectoriesToAlwaysStageAsNonUFS`) because the media player (AvfMedia) opens it as a file. It is footage of the game
itself: `sr.Film record` plays the shots in `Saved/Film.txt` (the take in use is kept as
`SourceArt/Movies/LoadingBattle.film.txt`) and saves every frame at a fixed 30 fps of game time, holding the game's
clock until each frame is on disk; `Tools/film.py` cross-fades the shots and makes the end dissolve into the start, so
the loop has no seam. `sr.Host` and `sr.March` stage the armies; `sr.Demo` is the old single-shot version.

**Demolishing.** A finished building of yours (not the Citadel) can be pulled down: the Demolish tab on its panel's top
edge, or Delete, twice (the first press asks and shows what comes back). It slumps into rubble as a fallen one does,
and `DemolishRefund` (30%) of everything paid for it comes back: its price and every level it was raised to; recruits in
its queue are refunded in full. A storehouse pulled down takes its capacity with it (stock over what the kingdom can
still hold is lost). A site still going up is cancelled instead (Delete or Cancel: `CancelRefund`, 75%).
`ASRBuilding::Demolish`, `sr.SelectBuildingAt X Y` for tests.

**The castle's zone and Builders.** The Citadel has a building zone of 42 m (`TerritoryRadius`; the dashed gold ring
shown while placing or with the castle selected). A building laid inside it raises itself as before. Outside it, the
kingdom must have a Builder: the building is laid as a bare site and only rises while a Builder works at its walls
(the selected Builders go to it, or else the nearest free one; each extra Builder adds 50% speed, up to 4). Builders
also speed up sites inside the zone and repair damaged buildings (a building brought back above half health loses
its fire and damaged look). An idle Builder of yours goes to work on its own on a site or a badly damaged building
within 26 m; right-click one of your sites or damaged buildings with Builders selected to send them. Builders do not take housing (their own limit is the castle's level). The top bar's Builder counter opens a list of
them with what each is doing; click one to select it and fly there. Builders are
dear (120 gold + 80 food, 14 s), capped by the castle's level (2 at level 1, up to 6; shown in the top bar, click it to select
them), never join platoons from recruitment, and never pick fights. The enemy commander keeps 1 (Easy) or 2 Builders
and uses them the same way. **Repair**: X (or the Repair button) sends the selected Builders to the nearest damaged
building; hovering one of your damaged buildings with Builders selected shows "RMB: repair it". **A building under
repair is out of action** until the Builders finish: it trains nothing, pays nothing (a worker's load is lost) and a
tower holds its fire (its panel says "Under repair: out of action"). So an idle Builder never starts mending on its own
a building hit in the last 10 s; ordered repairs (X, RMB) start at once.

**Dismiss** (K twice, or the Dismiss button twice: the first press asks): the selected troops are removed for good, no
refund, quietly (no cry, no shake). Meant for a troop that is stuck.

**Raising the Citadel** (U, or the Upgrade button on the castle; `CastleLevels`, 7 levels): the castle is how a kingdom
grows. Each level raises the castle's own housing, the kingdom's troop ceiling (however many farms it builds), the
Builder limit, the building zone, the walls and the stores (gold and food beyond them are lost; the top bar shows
"have / hold", amber when full). The stores always hold the next level's price.

**Storehouses: one Granary and one Treasury** (`StoreLimitBase` 1, `StoreLimitCastleLevels` 0); instead of building more,
upgrade them: level 2 needs the Citadel at level 4, level 3 at level 5 (`FSRStoreLevel::RequiredCastleLevel`; the
upgrade button shows "Citadel level 4" in red until then).

**Raising the Citadel shows a card** of what the new level brings (Clash of Clans' town hall upgrade, which lists the
new buildings and levels it unlocks, was the model): troop ceiling, Builders, how many Watchtowers and Farms, stores,
building zone and walls, and anything NEW at exactly that level (the Scout at 3, storehouse levels at 4 and 5). It
fades after 14 s or on "Onward". The upgrade button's tooltip lists the NEW things and the extra buildings beforehand.

**Watchtowers are limited** by the castle's level: 2 at level 1 and one more per level (`TowerLimitBase`,
`TowerLimitPerCastleLevel`; sites count). The build menu shows "used / allowed" on the tower; at the limit it is refused
("Watchtower limit 2 reached: raise the Citadel for another"). The enemy commander keeps to the same limit.

**Storehouses: the Granary (food) and the Treasury (gold)** (`ESRBuildingType::Granary / Treasury`, `FSRStoreLevel`,
`GranaryLevels` / `TreasuryLevels`; Economy tab of the build menu). The castle's level sets what a kingdom can hold;

level 3 holds a little more than a level-3 castle does on its own. Upgrade with the button on its panel or U; its panel

- **Limit**: one Granary and one Treasury (`StoreLimitBase` 1; `StoreLimitCastleLevels` 0 means it never grows; sites
  count, ruins do not). They are upgraded instead: level 2 from Citadel level 4, level 3 from level 5. At castle level 7
  with a level-3 storehouse a kingdom holds 5500 + 1600 = 7100 of that resource.
- **A storehouse under construction or being upgraded** counts at the level it has finished (nothing until it is
  built; the old capacity until the upgrade is done). One under repair still holds its stock.
- **If a storehouse is destroyed, what it held goes with it**: the kingdom's stock of that resource is cut down to
  what its remaining stores hold, at once, and the rest is lost (the alert says how much:
  "Granary destroyed: 240 food lost with it"; `USREconomySubsystem::NotifyBuildingDestroyed`). Stock that still fits
  is untouched. So a full Granary or Treasury is worth raiding, and worth a tower.
- **The enemy commander** builds a Granary when its food reaches 90% of its stores and a Treasury when its gold does
  (after its more pressing wants: troops' buildings, housing, towers), and enlarges one while that resource is above
  85% of its stores; it keeps to the same limit.
- **Look and sound**: the Granary is a plank barn on staddle stones under a steep thatch, sacks and baskets round it
  (`Tools/blender/bld_granary.py`); the Treasury a squat bronze-banded stone vault with a bronze dome, braziers at the
  door and chests of coin (`bld_treasury.py`). Selecting a Granary plays the grain thump, a Treasury the coins; in
  life the Granary has sparrows and the odd sack set down, the Treasury a clerk counting coin.

Builders at the walls speed the raising up; the new walls come up whole. The enemy commander raises its castle when its
housing runs short or its gold stores are nearly full, and it can pay. Saved games keep the level. The build menu (B, or the Build button) groups them in tabs: Economy (farm, mine, granary, treasury), Military (barracks, archery range),
Defence (watchtower); 1-6 picks, Tab changes tab. Placing pays the gold; the building rises course by
course over its build time with hammering, and works when done. Delete / Backspace cancels a building under
construction for 75% of its price. Destroyed buildings slump into rubble (runtime ones clear away; the level's own
stand again on restart).

## The Scout

`ESRUnitType::Scout` (Sovereign Feel > Units > Scout, and > Scouts). A hooded runner with a light spear, trained at the
**Citadel** (the second recruit button on its panel, or **J**) for 200 food + 350 gold in 12 s; it takes 1 housing.

- **Three rules, all checked when it is queued** (`USREconomySubsystem::GetScoutRefusal`, `ASRBuilding::QueueRecruit`):
  1. **Citadel level 3** (`ScoutCastleLevel`). Below it the button is greyed and reads "Citadel level 3".
  2. **One at a time** (`MaxScouts` 1): alive plus in training. The button reads "In training" or "In the field".
  3. **A 5-minute wait** (`ScoutCooldown` 300 s of game time) between two Scouts. The clock starts the moment a Scout
     is queued and paid for, so losing or dismissing it does not shorten the wait. The button counts down
     ("Ready in 3:12"; "In the field · 3:12" while the last one lives).
  A refused press says why in the alert line ("A Scout needs the Citadel at level 3", "You already have a Scout (a
  kingdom has only one at a time)", "The next Scout can be trained in 3:12"). No batches. The enemy commander is held
  to the same three rules; the wait is saved with the game (`USRSaveGame::ScoutCooldown`).
- **Fast and far-sighted**: 8.2 m/s (a Horse Rider 6.4, a Shieldbearer 3.9), sight 26 m (an Archer 15 m), so it lifts a
  wide stretch of the fog as it runs. 90 health, 8 damage a blow, 0.2x against buildings: one volley finishes it.
- **It does not pick fights** (no acquire range): it strikes what it is ordered to attack and hits back while it is
  being hit, lets go 4 s after the last blow, and does not answer other troops' calls for help. It does not join the
  recruit platoon (it can be put in a platoon by hand).
- **Reduced visibility, not invisibility** (`FSRUnitStats::NoticeScale`, 0.45 for the Scout; 1 for everyone else).
  Everything that would notice a troop at distance D notices the Scout only within 0.45 x D:
  - enemy troops auto-acquire it at 45% of their acquire range (a Shieldbearer at 5.9 m instead of 13 m, an Archer at
    6.8 m instead of 15 m), and lose it again at 45% of the distance they would hold another target to
    (`USRBattleSubsystem::FindNearestEnemy`, `ASRUnit::UpdateTargeting`);
  - watchtowers and the castle's walls shoot it only inside 45% of their reach (a level-1 tower at 10.8 m instead of
    24 m; `ASRBuilding::StepDefence`);
  - the **fog of war works the same way against the player**: an enemy Scout standing on ground the player can see
    is still hidden (world, minimap, picking, health bar) until one of the player's troops is within 45% of its own
    sight, or a building within 45% of what it sees (`USRFogSubsystem::IsUnitVisible`);
  - the enemy commander's sight memory and its "foes at my buildings" alarm use the same fraction
    (`USRAISubsystem::Observe`, `ManageDefence`).
  A direct order (right-click it) is not affected: what the player can see, the player can attack. Auto-battle
  (`sr.AutoBattle`), where both sides see the whole map, ignores it.
- **The look**: a deep hood and a long cloak in the team's colour (`Tools/blender/char_worker.py`, variant `scout`;
  the Builder's body, rig and sheet layout, with a sprinter's 192 cm run cycle: `FSRUnitStats::WalkCycleDistance`),
  and its sprite is drawn thinned out, about a quarter of its texels dropped in a dither (`StealthSpriteFade` 0.72),
  like something half seen. It is drawn whole while it strikes and for the moment it is hit. Until
  `T_Scout_*` / `MI_UnitSprite_Scout` are imported it borrows the Builder's sheet (same layout).
- **The enemy's Scout** (Normal and Hard, once it has six troops): trained when the three rules allow, kept out of
  attacks and the defence, and sent every 16-28 s to a new look-out beside one of the player's buildings (the castle
  most often), far enough out not to be noticed; below 60% health it goes home and stays (`USRAISubsystem::ManageScout`).

## Movement and orders

Deep water is a hard wall: no step, shove or knock-back takes a troop into it (`bWaterWalker` would let one wade;
none does yet); the river is crossed at its fords. `ASRUnit::CanStep` and `ASRUnit::Nudge` ask both the probe ahead
and the spot the troop would land on (a step along a bank can clear a water cell's corner with the probe alone), a
move order's goal and every formation slot are snapped to walkable ground (`USRGridSubsystem::NearestWalkable`),
building footprints refuse water, and `SpawnUnit` moves a spawn out of deep water. `Tools/water_wall_test.py
<two|four>` (PIE running on that map) orders squads straight at and across every body of water and starts fights on
the banks; `sr.Status`'s `inwater=` must stay 0.

Every order goes through `USRBattleSubsystem::CommandMove / CommandAttack / CommandAttackBuilding`. A group move lays
out formation slots at the goal (`SRFormation::ComputeSlots`: Line, Column, Wedge, Square) and asks the grid for paths:
**one** search from the goal serves the whole group, each member string-pulls its own route to a few corners. Routes
keep a cell clear of walls wherever there is room: the search pays extra for cells hard against a wall, and the
straightening runs only through open cells (or wall-side cells the search itself chose: a gap, a ramp's lip), so a
route round a hill is not pulled tight against its cliff. Formation slots are fitted to ground that can be walked to
from the goal (`FitSlots`: none across a cliff edge, in water or in a wall). Units still steer locally (separation,
sidestepping, sliding along walls); one that makes no headway toward its next corner for about a second plans the
rest of the march again from where it stands (`ASRUnit::Repath`, up to five times an order) instead of skipping the
corner. A unit chasing a target that is out of straight reach re-plans a grid path about once a second. Units attack
and Builders work on buildings by a route to whichever wall is nearest on foot (`FindPathToBuilding`), not the wall
nearest as the crow flies.

**Formation**: F (or clicking the Form button, or a platoon card's formation button) switches it on or off.
Off, the group moves loose: each keeps its place relative to the others, shifted to the destination, and a group spread
wide is drawn in so it does not arrive scattered (`ESRFormationShape::Loose`). Right-clicking the button picks the next
shape (Line > Column > Wedge > Square), which also switches it on; switched on, the group re-forms where it stands at
once, facing the way it faces. A selection that is not a platoon has its own setting too; platoons save theirs. In
formation a group always keeps its slowest troop's pace on the march (the March together setting only matters with
formation off). **Patrol**: P, then a click: the group attack-moves back and forth between where it stood and the point
until it is given another order. P, then Shift+click several points: a patrol round all of them and back to the start
(P stays armed while Shift is held; `ExtendPatrol`). **Pathway**: Shift+right-click a point, then more: the group walks
them in order, a leg at a time in formation, and stops at the last (`ExtendPath`). Routes are drawn on the ground while
the group is selected (a patrol's loop in orange, the pathway's legs still to walk in blue).

**The Medic** (Barracks, N; 220 gold + 140 food, 18 s, 1 housing; Sovereign Feel > Medics). Never fights. It treats one
wounded troop of its side at a time and must touch it: it runs there (540 cm/s, quicker than foot troops), kneels and
bandages, +30 health every 1.5 s, until that troop is whole, then goes to the next (the worst hurt first). On its own it
looks for the wounded round it: Assertive (G) as far as 14 m, into the fight; Passive (the default) only 9 m and only
where no foe is near, and it runs from any foe within 8.5 m, even from the troop it looks after, coming back once
clear. Right-click one of your troops with Medics selected: they look after that troop, following it and treating it
and the wounded within 7 m of it. On the march (a move order) it does not stop to heal. Holding ground, it treats only
what is within touch. A green "+30" rises over a troop it heals; the solid ring under a selected Medic is how far it
goes on its own. `sr.Spawn medic X Y`, `sr.Heal X Y` (the selected Medics look after the nearest troop), `sr.Hurt
<fraction>`, `sr.OrderPath X Y`, `sr.PatrolPoint X Y`.

**Trackpad** (Mac): the Mac reports a two-finger scroll and a pinch as gestures, not wheel turns
(`ASRPlayerController::InputGesture`). Two-finger scroll pans the map 1:1 (the ground follows the fingers, whichever
way System Settings sets the scroll direction); Settings > Trackpad scroll can make it zoom instead. Pinch zooms toward
the pointer. Control+click on the battlefield is a right-click (`bControlClickIsRightClick`). `sr.Trackpad scroll DX DY`
and `sr.Trackpad pinch M` feed it a gesture for tests. Option+scroll zooms (trackpad or Magic Mouse). **Fog of war** can be switched off in Settings.

**Line of sight**: a troop notices (and, in Aggressive stance or on an attack-move, goes for) enemy troops and buildings
within its sight: 13 m for Shieldbearers, 15 m for Archers (bow range 8.8 m). Selected troops show it on the ground: a
dashed blue outline for sight, a solid orange one for archers' bow range (one merged outline per group; Sovereign Feel
> Combat > Show Range Circles). A selected watchtower or castle shows its reach the same way (red for the enemy's),
and so does a tower while you are placing it.

**Guard** (V then a click, the Guard button, or right-click): the selected troops guard one of your Builders or one
of your buildings. Troops cannot be guarded, only Builders; Builders in the selection do not guard (right-clicking your
own building sends them to build or repair it while the troops guard it). Each guard takes a post in a ring round the
ward (`GuardRingRadius` round a Builder, just outside the walls of a building) and keeps to it as the Builder walks;
it fights foes within `GuardLeash` (14 m) of the ward, runs down one that struck the ward a little further (x1.8), and
gives up a chase that takes it too far. A Builder that is hit calls its guards in. The order ends when the ward falls.

**Stances** (per platoon or per selection, G cycles). Every troop not told otherwise attacks any enemy troop or building
in its line of sight (its sight range; troops before buildings).
- **Aggressive**: chases what it goes for, well past its sight (it lets go at twice its sight range unless struck).
- **Defensive**: chases only to the edge of its sight from its post (`max(DefensiveChaseDistance, sight)`), then walks
  back to the post.
- **Hold Ground**: never moves to fight, not even an archer stepping back: strikes only what is already in reach (H gives
  a one-off hold order).

**Roam** (Scouts only: O or the Roam button): the Scout wanders on its own, picking spots 20-70 m off, ground never
explored first, then ground out of sight; a leg that takes over 40 s is dropped for a new one. Struck by anything
hostile (a tower's arrow too) it drops the roam and runs home to the Citadel's gate, with an alert.

## Platoons

`USRPlatoonSubsystem`: six slots. Ctrl+N makes the selection platoon N, N selects it (twice: centre the camera),
Shift+N adds to it. **March together** (M, or the March button; on by default, per platoon and for loose selections):
on the march every member keeps the slowest member's pace, so riders do not leave the shields behind; a fight met on the
way is fought at full speed. Each platoon slot has its own colour (gold, cyan, magenta, orange, white, violet...): a ring under
every member, a numbered tag over the platoon, its pips when zoomed far out, and its badge in the panel
(`bShowPlatoonColors`). A platoon has a name, a kind judged from its members (Shield Wall, Archer Company, Mixed Host), a
formation (F cycles) used whenever it is moved, a stance pushed to its members, and an optional "recruits join here"
flag (new troops from the barracks and ranges join it and march to it). Disband: the button on its card, or Delete
with it selected (the troops stay). A platoon whose last troop falls is struck off by itself and its slot is free.

**A training building can feed a platoon**: its panel's "Joins" row (none, 1-6), or Ctrl+N with the building selected
(again: none). Every troop it trains then joins platoon N and marches to it; a platoon that was wiped out is formed
again by the next recruit. This comes before the platoon's "recruits join here" flag. Disbanding the platoon clears the
buildings that fed it. Saved with the game.

**Rally points**: select a Barracks or Archery Range and right-click the ground (or the minimap): its recruits march
there. The route is drawn while it is selected. Q / E train at the selected one, or at your least busy one.

## The enemy commander

`USRAISubsystem` (Sovereign Feel > AI). It plays by the player's rules and knows only what it has seen: every think
it notes the player's troops within 26 m of its own troops and buildings and remembers them for 90 s (buildings are
known). Strength is estimated per troop as sqrt(health x damage per second), archers x1.5 for their reach, scaled by
health left; towers and the castle count as defenders where they reach. Difficulty never adds income.
- **Economy**: a build order (barracks, farm, range, mine, farm, tower, ...), then whatever is short: farms for housing
  and food, mines on free veins, towers (more if it has been attacked), a second barracks / range when food piles up;
  a lost barracks or range is rebuilt first. A building that finds no room is skipped for 25 s, so one bad spot never
  stalls the economy.
- **Adapts to the player**: trains toward a Shieldbearer share that answers what it has seen (more Shieldbearers
  against archers and towers, more Archers against a wall of shields); a bigger garrison and earlier towers against a
  player who attacks it; mines are worth more as targets when the player has more of them.
- **Defence**: meets a force near its buildings with the nearest troops until it has 1.6x their strength (its towers
  count); outmatched at home, it recalls its raid, and its main attack too unless that attack is about to win (from
  anywhere if the castle itself is threatened: no blind base races).
- **Attack sense**: picks the target worth most for the least expected resistance (economy first, a half-built or
  damaged building gladly, the castle when its defence is beatable) and goes when its free army is 1.3x (Easy 1.7x,
  Hard 1.1x) what it expects to meet there: troops seen nearby, the unseen rest of the player's army near the
  player's castle, and towers. It also goes when it is at its housing cap, or 150 s past due at nearly even odds. A
  winning attack is reinforced every 20 s; one whose local fight turns (below 0.65x) falls back to regroup; after a
  target falls it re-picks from where it stands, and comes home if the next is too strong.
- **Raids**: every 75 s (Hard 52 s) a party of 3-4 Shieldbearers slips at an outlying farm or mine where it has seen
  next to no defence (never near the castle), burns it and comes home, or turns back the moment it is outmatched.
- **Difficulty** (`USRAISubsystem::Profile`; the AI timing / odds settings are the Normal values):

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Income (share of its farms' and mines' output) | 60% | 90% | 100% |
| Earliest attack | 7:00 | 4:00 | 2:24 |
| Odds it waits for | 2.0x | 1.5x | 1.15x |
| Regroup between attacks | 153 s | 90 s | 40 s |
| Attack size (first, +per attack, max) | 5, +2, 14 | 8, +3, 24 | 10, +4, 30 |
| Raids | none | every 120 s from 6:00 | every 60 s from 2:24 |
| Adapts its army to yours | no | 60% | fully |
| Reinforces / attacks at even odds after waiting | no | yes | yes |
| Towers / farms at most, decisions every | 2 / 6, 1.5 s | 4 / 10, 1 s | 7 / 14, 0.6 s |

  Passive-player tests (`sr.Speed 8`): Easy attacked first at 7:00 with 5 troops and twice in 20 minutes, the castle
  still standing; Normal attacked at ~6:30 and took the castle at ~10 minutes.

## Balance, as tested (accelerated runs, `sr.Speed 8`)

- **AI vs AI, Normal** (`sr.AIvsAI 1`): raids from ~3 min, the first attacks trade mines; both sides adapt (each
  settled on 65% Shieldbearers), recall attacks to defend, and at 22 minutes it was still contested (7 attacks vs 4,
  8 raids vs 7). Earlier builds ended in a base race at 5 minutes (fixed: a threatened castle recalls the attack, and
  buildings got health to match the stronger troops) and in a side that never attacked again after an attack was
  wiped out (fixed).
- **Passive player**: see the difficulty table above. A player who builds a barracks and a tower holds the early
  attacks at Normal.
- Towers: 40 damage an arrow: a Shieldbearer dies to 7, an Archer to 4; three towers make an assault expensive.

## Fog of war, saves, effects

- **Fog of war** (`USRFogSubsystem`, post-process `M_FogOfWar` from `Tools/build_fog_material.py`): the player sees
  what their troops (line of sight) and buildings see. Enemy troops outside it are hidden (world, minimap, picking,
  health bars); an enemy building stays hidden until first seen, then is remembered. Unexplored land is dark,
  explored land dimmed and greyed. `sr.Fog 0|1` toggles it for tests. The enemy commander keeps its own sight memory.
  An enemy Scout shows only from much closer (see The Scout).
- **Saved games** (`USRGameFlowSubsystem::SaveMatch / LoadMatch`, `USRSaveGame`): three slots of the player's own
  (`MaxPlayerSaves`) plus the autosave. The pause menu's SAVE GAME opens the list with NEW SAVE (a slot of its own,
  `SR_Save_<date>_<time>`; disabled once all three are in use) and OVERWRITE on each save (at once over this match's
  own save, a second click to go over another game's); LOAD GAME (pause menu, and under CONTINUE on the title screen)
  opens it with LOAD. Every row, the autosave too, has DELETE (click twice: the first asks "SURE?"). Newest first.
  **Quitting or restarting** asks first and knows whether the match is saved: saved (or just loaded) and not played
  on since, it says so and offers CANCEL / QUIT; otherwise it says how long ago the last save was and offers CANCEL /
  DON'T SAVE / SAVE & QUIT. SAVE & QUIT goes over the match's own save, else into a free slot; with all three slots
  holding other games the list comes up to choose which one to overwrite, and the quit follows that save.
  The list is read from the platform's save folder (`ISaveGameSystem::GetSaveGameNames`: slots starting `SR_Save`, and
  `SR_Autosave`). One autosave every 2 minutes, overwritten each time; CONTINUE loads the newest save. Saved: troops (type, place, health, stance, platoon), buildings
  (construction, damage, ruins, queues, rally points; the level's by name, the match's re-spawned), resources,
  platoons, explored fog, the commander's progress, the clock, the camera, each kingdom's wait before its next Scout.
  Storehouses and their levels are saved like any building's. `sr.Save` / `sr.Load`.
- **Behind buildings**: troops render custom depth with the team in the stencil; `M_Occlusion`
  (`Tools/build_occlusion_material.py`) shows a hidden troop as a team-coloured checker silhouette.
- **Effects** (`ASRFXSprite`, strips in `SourceArt/FX`, instances `MI_FX_*`): a building below half health burns and
  smokes, a ruin smokes for 25 s, struck walls throw chips, catapult stones burst, fallen troops and galloping horses
  raise dust.
- **Zoomed far out**, a team-coloured pip marks every visible troop.

## Sound

Every sound is a recording: the user's own (`SourceArt/Audio/Recorded`) and CC0 sounds from Kenney and OpenGameArt
(`SourceArt/Audio/Recorded/cc0`, sources in its `CREDITS.md`). `Tools/audio/prep_recorded.py` turns them into
`SourceArt/Audio/S_<Name>[_N].wav` (48 kHz mono, peak-normalised, trimmed; several takes of a sound are picked at random)
and `Tools/import_audio.py` brings them into `/Game/SR/Audio`. The old synthesiser (`Tools/audio/gen_sounds.py`) is
retired. Combat: six sword clashes, six shield blocks, punchy flesh hits, wood and dirt arrow hits; orders: a belt and
leather creak on select, a boot's thud with a soft wooden click on a move order, a blade on an attack order; Kenney interface click / error;
coins and grain-sack payouts; timber set down for a new site, a gate opening for a recruit, two mallet knocks and a
small bell when a building is done, wood and stone hits and a layered timber-and-rubble collapse for buildings; picks
and soil for the workers; a fanfare for victory and a falling steel phrase for defeat; a 63 s morning-countryside
ambience loop.
Recorded sounds (`SourceArt/Audio/Recorded`, turned into game sounds by `Tools/audio/prep_recorded.py`): two bow
releases (picked at random), an arrow whistle that rides on every arrow (pitched down, on catapult stones), a sword
swing on every melee wind-up, two horse snorts (idle riders, now and then) and a gallop loop cut from a pass-by that
plays under riders at speed (three at most at once) and fades in and out as they start and stop. A burning (damaged)
building roars with a fire loop (built from a recorded crackle: shaped low noise plus crackle grains, seamless) that
fades in when it catches and out when it is repaired or falls, with a recorded flare-up every few seconds. A destroyed
catapult bursts apart (recorded timber break). A Builder's mallet blows are recorded hammer strikes. Foot troops on the move (three or more walking together, and
seen) tramp with a recorded march loop that follows the group's middle and grows with its size (the nearest three
groups; it fades in as they set off and away as they halt). A rider neighs leaving the stable and, often, as its
charge lands. A troop falls with one of four recorded grunts.

**Building voices and life** (CC0 sounds from Kenney and OpenGameArt; sources in
`SourceArt/Audio/Recorded/cc0/CREDITS.md`): selecting one of your buildings plays its voice: the Citadel a heavy bell,
a farm a sheep, a mine a pick on rock, a watchtower creaking timber, the barracks a drawn blade, the archery range an
arrow into a target, the stable a neigh, the siege workshop a saw, the granary a thump of grain, the treasury coins. Standing buildings have life every 6-15 s, heard
close by: the farm's sheep and birds, the barracks' armourer at the anvil, the range's targets, the stable's snorts,
the workshop's sawing and chopping, the odd creak of a tower. Sites going up mix saw strokes into the hammering. A
catapult's arm groans as it winds back and its stone lands with a heavy crash. Six or more troops fighting near the
camera raise a battle roar (a crowd loop) that follows the fight and swells with it. Zoomed far out, the wind rises.
`USRAudioSubsystem` plays them positioned from a listener over the camera's focus (so screen-left is heard on the
left) that rises with the zoom (`ListenerZoomHeight`), with a minimum gap and voice cap per sound so a big melee stays
a clatter. **Hearing range**: every positioned sound has its own range (pickaxe 18 m, arrow whistle 20 m, sword 32 m,
building hit 40 m, a collapse 90 m; `SoundRangeScale` scales them all). Inside a fifth of it a sound is at full
volume, then it fades on a smooth curve to silence exactly at the range, so nothing cuts off as the camera moves away:
a playing sound (a gallop, a hammer) fades out as you pass its edge, and zoomed far out only the big sounds carry.
Sounds wholly out of range are not started.

**Alerts**: the player is not told when the enemy sets off; they learn of an attack when it lands: "Your <building> is
under attack!" (each building at most every 10 s) and "Your troops are under attack!" when troops are hit out of view
(at most every 15 s, not on top of a building alert from the same fight), each with a short notification tone (no
horn). Clicking the alert goes there. Over each of your buildings at work a bar shows its progress: construction (red
"needs a Builder" while a site outside the castle's zone waits), the castle being raised, repairs, and training. Volumes: Sovereign Feel > Audio and the settings menu.

## Shadow flicker

Cause: the virtual shadow map cache was being invalidated every frame by anything with world-position offset (the
swaying firs and grass, the troops' billboard cards), made worse since UE 5.7 by the directional receiver mask.
Fixes: `r.Shadow.Virtual.UseReceiverMaskDirectional=0` (`Config/DefaultEngine.ini`); the foliage instances cast
cached shadows rigidly (`ShadowCacheInvalidationBehavior = Rigid`) and stop evaluating sway beyond 90 m
(`Tools/build_battlefield_visuals.py`). Measured with consecutive still frames: large pixel changes fell from 0.065% to
0.008-0.025% of the frame; what remains is the intended gentle sway.

## Performance

Measured with `sr.Perf <seconds>` (averages frame / game / render / GPU time). M5 Pro, 1920x1080.

| Case | Editor PIE | Standalone game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Match start (17 troops) | 76 fps (GPU 13.1 ms) | **88 fps** (GPU 11.3 ms, game 0.9 ms) |
| Mid-match, ~106 troops, 34 buildings, default zoom | 58 fps | **87 fps** (GPU 11.0 ms, game 2.4 ms) |
| Same, fully zoomed out | 61 fps | **92 fps** (GPU 10.3 ms, game 3.1 ms) |

The editor's own UI costs ~2-3 ms of GPU, so judge performance in a standalone run
(`UnrealEditor <project> -game -windowed -ResX=1920 -ResY=1080 -SRSkipMenu`).

What was done (following Epic's significance / instancing guidance and profiling with `ProfileGPU`):
- **Neighbour searches**: a bucket grid of units rebuilt every step (`USRBattleSubsystem::QueryUnits`) replaces the
  all-pairs loops in avoidance, overlap resolution, targeting and alerts (O(n²) -> O(nearby)).
- **Significance culling**: troops and workers more than ~1.6x the camera distance from its focus are hidden and skip
  all per-frame presentation (transforms, sprite cells, shadow cards); simulation is untouched.
- **Instancing**: grass, stones, deadwood and firs are hierarchical instanced components with cull distances (grass
  130 m, stones 180 m); all blocker boxes are one instanced actor instead of hundreds of actors.
- **GPU**: tilt-shift recombine quality 0 (-1.2 ms; it was the single most expensive pass), volumetric fog back to the
  default froxel grid now that the haze is thin (-~1 ms).
- **Pathfinding**: one grid search per group order (not per unit), straight lines when the way is clear, chase
  re-planning throttled to ~1 s.

## Four kingdoms: what real four-sided play needs

The Four Marches is built for four kingdoms and played by two. The map side is done (four equal marches, quarter-turn
symmetry, tables, home veins, roads and contested ground for every pair of neighbours); the game is two-sided
throughout. To seat four:

- **Teams**: `ESRTeam` is Player / Enemy, and "the foe" is computed as "the other one" in many places
  (`USRAISubsystem::Foe`, targeting, the fog, alerts, the end-of-match screen). It needs a team id (0..3) with a
  relation table (hostile / allied), four team colours (`TEAM_COLORS` in the level builder, the sprite materials'
  team tint, minimap and ring colours) and per-team unit lists.
- **Economy and saves**: stocks, incomes, Scout cool-downs, match statistics and the saved game's `Gold[2]`, `Food[2]`,
  `Commanders[2]`, `Stats` are arrays of two; make them per team (bump `USRSaveGame::CurrentVersion`).
- **Commanders**: one `FSRCommander` per rival. Each needs a choice of whom to attack (nearest, weakest, the leader)
  where today the target is always the one foe, and a muster point toward that rival rather than "toward the foe".
- **Starts**: `ASRMapInfo` carries `PlayerStart` / `EnemyStart`; it needs a start per kingdom (the layout already
  lists them: `layout4.json` > `map.kingdoms`, each with its anchor), and the level builder must place a castle,
  farm and mine for every seated kingdom (`maps.kingdom` does it per team; the two fallen keeps and their relics
  would be left out for seats that are taken).
- **Winning**: the match ends when one castle falls; with four it should end when one side (or alliance) is left,
  and a fallen kingdom's buildings and troops need a fate (razed, or handed to the victor).
- **Fog of war and the interface**: the fog is the player's view against one enemy; the HUD's enemy counts, alerts
  ("the enemy attacks"), the minimap's colours and the New Game screen (pick a seat, pick rivals and their
  difficulty, teams) assume one rival.
- **Balance**: neighbours are 443 m apart on foot and opposite corners 633 m; the Normal commander's first-attack
  time and wave sizes were tuned for 273 m.
